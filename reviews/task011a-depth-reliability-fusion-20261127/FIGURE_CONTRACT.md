Core conclusion: the observed rank fusion improves continuous error ranking but trades off event discrimination; these are three reused frames in one MVS sequence, with no certified VIPER depth accuracy or independent probability-calibration claim.

Evidence chain: (1) four primary metric panels with each image visible, (2) risk-coverage/error-decile curves and the fixed sensitivity weights, (3) calibration diagrams with constant controls, (4) actual aligned input/risk/error plates, (5) VIPER raw-signal/overlap shift with the absence of admitted GT explicit. Do not hide negative results or normalize away raw-weather score differences.

Archetypes: quantitative grid for metrics, image plate plus quantitative source table for risk maps. Quantitative figures export editable SVG/PDF plus PNG; diagnostic image plates export PNG with all pixels/aspect retained, common percentile normalization 0–1, and an explicitly display-clipped AbsRel color scale. Sampling/statistics: three previously observed evaluation images in one sequence; no pixel-IID significance or cross-sequence confidence interval. Show individual image values and sample SD across images, clearly not a CI. VIPER has two separated samples per weather, not a continuous-time or causal-weather experiment.

Integrity: never show a surrogate error map for VIPER. Native confidence and percentile ranks are not probabilities. Common color ranges may not be used to imply common physical units. Every plot has a source CSV; raw float64 rank maps remain in the full review package.
